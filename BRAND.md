## Using this foundation

- Read this file first, then the audience and channel guidance relevant to the task.
- This file governs brand intent, promises, and boundaries. Specialist guidelines define execution; asset files hold exact visual resources and values. Product specifications govern factual details, which brand language must not override. Examples illustrate rules and do not override them.
- Direct instructions set requirements. **Prefer** marks a default that can adapt to the context. **Example** marks an illustration, not a product fact or testimonial.
- If guidance conflicts, apply this foundation to the brand decision and flag the conflicting passage for correction. If a required fact, asset, or rule is missing, name the gap. Continue with supported work; label exploratory choices as proposals rather than established brand standards.

## Brand core

### Essence

**We care.**
The child's own discovery is the moment we want to make possible while respecting the need of parents for realistic and simple solutions suiting their day-to-day family life.

### Mission — why we exist

To give children more opportunities to experience their own capability, with support from the adults around them.

### Vision — the future we want

A generation of confident people who are empowered and curious to explore, try, and learn.

### What empowerment means for us

A child has room to choose, attempt, discover, and ask for help. Adults provide support appropriate to the child and situation. Independence must not become pressure to manage alone, reach a milestone early, or perform for an adult.

## Audiences

- **Market:** German-speaking families across Germany, Austria, and Switzerland.
- **Child audience:** Ages **2–6**; named interview personas aged **3–6**. Siblings aged 0–7 provide household context, not additional targeting. Audience age is not a product age rating.
- **Perspective:** Whole family life: work, care, friendships, play, meals, errands, personal interests, downtime, and weekends.
- **Commercial context:** Typical intended product range **€20–40**, supplied by the founder. No fixed household-income cutoff, buyer-gender ratio, validated willingness-to-pay threshold, or assumed Swiss retail price.

| Audience and role | Recognizable tension | What the brand must offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Children: participants and users | Wants to carry the bag; hands it back when it becomes heavy; still wants a meaningful part | An understandable invitation, room to choose and try, and help without losing ownership of the attempt |
| Parents and caregivers: people, purchasers, and supporters | Wants to let the child finish; takes over when departure time arrives; also wants work, friends, hobbies, and enjoyable family time | A clear purpose, proportionate effort, honest expectations, and respect for different household arrangements |

- **Examples:** Fictional illustrations of tensions, not observed customer stories.
- **Design for both:** A parent's ease and a child's active participation must work together; do not treat the child as a problem to control.
- **Start with the [audience overview](personas/README.md):** Five fictional families across DACH; [parent profiles](personas/parents/parents.md), [child profiles](personas/children/children.md), and a [research register](personas/research.md).
- **Evidence:** Distinguish research findings, brand decisions, hypotheses, and fictional persona details. Sources inform patterns; they do not establish the cast's biographies, market prevalence, or product effectiveness.
- **Interview agents:** The adjacent persona `AGENT.md` files apply only to explicitly requested hypothetical interviews; identify outputs as simulations, never testimonials or customer validation.

## Positioning and promises

### Internal positioning

For parents across German-speaking DACH who want to support their children's independence within busy daily life, selba develops simple, playful tools that let children take an active role in everyday challenges. Our approach brings together familiar play, research-informed development, and ease of use for families.

Research-informed development is a commitment to the process in our mission. Any public claim that a product is research-backed needs a documented basis.

Our intended distinction is the combination: **simple enough for a busy day, with room for the child to lead.** Show it through the interaction, setup, and explanation of each product. 

### Promises

| To whom | What we commit to designing for | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parents | Useful tools that are easy to understand and fit into everyday life | Do not promise effortless parenting, a guilt-free purchase, or a guaranteed change in a child |
| Children | Space to explore, try, and grow through their own participation | Do not equate success with obedience, speed, or doing everything alone |

These promises guide product and communication decisions. Specific statements about what a product does require supporting facts.

### The problem we address

Address the tension between parents needing something manageable and children needing space to participate. Critique specific problems or product behaviors when relevant. Do not shame families for using screens, familiar toys, or whatever helps them get through the day.

## Values in practice

| Value | Apply it | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple Creativity** | Find an inventive solution with a clear purpose, while removing unnecessary steps, features, and explanation. | Novelty that adds effort; complexity disguised as sophistication; careless execution |
| **Patience** | Give children time to try again. Give product development time for learning and refinement. | Pressure to progress faster; rushing an untested idea into a confident claim |
| **Problems First** | Identify the situation, fall in love with the problem and understand the need before proposing a feature. | Inventing a parental worry to justify a product; starting with a feature and searching for a need |
| **Impact** | We're taking our customer's potential and our potential serious and thrive for achieving our vision. | Attention capture or time spent as ends in themselves |
| **Growth** | We constantly have room for curiosity, changing abilities, and different approaches to scale and adapt our solutions for families out there. | Ranking children, fixed achievement timelines, or one supposedly correct way to parent |

### Resolving trade-offs

1. Protect trust, the child's dignity, and accurate information. Persuasion and playfulness must not obscure what matters.
2. Preserve the child's active role. An easier experience for the parent should still give the child something meaningful to do or choose.
3. Reduce effort for the family. Prefer the simpler option when both serve the same need.
4. Match the energy to the moment. Use playfulness to invite interest; use calm when the situation calls for reassurance or winding down.

## Brand expression

### Personality and voice

selba is the attentive guest who listens, makes room for others, and uses gentle humor to ease tension. We bring care and thought to the conversation without making ourselves the center of it.

Our voice is caring, patient, clear, and capable of quiet humor. Address parents by default in public copy; address children directly when they are the intended audience. Tone adapts to the task and the person's expressed situation:

| Situation | Direction | Example of tone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A child exploring | Be concrete, curious, and encouraging. Leave room for a choice. | “Möchtest du es selbst probieren?” |
| A parent considering a product | Explain the use, the parent's role, and the limits plainly. | “So nutzt ihr es im Alltag” as a heading, followed by verified steps |
| A parent asking for setup help | Answer the question or ask for the detail needed to help. Keep the language direct. | “Bei welchem Schritt kommst du nicht weiter?” |

These examples illustrate tone. Product copy still needs concrete, verified instructions and facts.

Use everyday language. Explain relevant ideas without lecturing parents or assuming how they feel. Humor is optional; leave it out in complaints, distress, and safety concerns. It must not come at the expense of a child or parent. Prefer German for the current German-speaking DACH audience unless the brief specifies another language. The [tone and voice guide](guidelines/tone-and-voice/tone-and-voice.md) defines the detailed writing rules.

### Name and core message

- Write the brand name **selba** in running text and headlines. The typeface is named **Selba**.
- The core message is: **“Guck mal, ich kann das schon selbaaaaa.”**
- Preserve that wording when using the core message. Its elongated ending expresses a child's pride and excitement; it is not the spelling of the brand name.
- Use the line where a moment of discovery or growing independence is relevant. It is not required on every touchpoint and must not replace clear instructions or factual product information.
- The line expresses the brand idea. Do not attribute it to a real child as a testimonial without a source.

### Visual direction

Make the experience inviting for children and understandable for parents. Use playful forms and recognizable cues with a clear hierarchy. Show care through legibility, spacing, and consistent execution.

- **Typography:** Selba is the expressive display typeface for brand and product titles. Figtree is the text typeface for other headings, body copy, navigation, controls, captions, and supporting information; it also serves as the fallback when Selba is unavailable. The [website design system](guidelines/DESIGN.md/website.md) defines the website’s exact roles, sizes, weights, and combinations. Other channel-specific execution belongs in the relevant design guidelines.
- **Color:** Use the documented palette and its Morning, Day, and Night groups. Exact values belong in the [color files](assets/colors/). Palette membership alone does not establish a readable text/background combination; check the actual use.
- **Imagery and illustration:** Prefer confident children participating, exploring, and trying, with support visible when appropriate. Avoid staging independence as a performance or presenting parents as inadequate. [imagery](assets/imagery/) and [illustrations](assets/illustrations/)
- **Logo and graphic assets:** Use supplied assets according to their documented rules. [logo](assets/logo/) and [icons](assets/icons/)

Trust comes from clear information and verifiable detail. Do not invent endorsements, scientific badges, or other symbols of authority to make a design feel credible.

## Claims and evidence

Distinguish the brand's intent from a product's demonstrated behavior. Use the narrowest statement the evidence supports.

| Statement type | Required basis |
| --- | --- |
| Brand intent, such as “We want to give children room to try” | Alignment with this foundation; do not present an intention as an achieved result |
| Product fact, such as materials, setup, age suitability, or compatibility | A current product specification or verified source for that exact product and version |
| Developmental or outcome claim | Evidence relevant to the specific claim, product, audience, and conditions; state material limits |
| Scientific rationale | An identifiable source and an explanation of how it informs the design; general research does not prove product effectiveness |
| Quote or testimonial | A traceable real source; never a persona, simulated interview, or invented review |

For each factual claim in a deliverable, retain the source alongside the work so it can be checked. If evidence is missing, omit the claim or explicitly describe the design intention. Adding “may” or “can” does not establish evidence for a benefit.

## Application check

Before handing over work, check:

- Is the audience and everyday need clear?
- Does the child have a meaningful role, with help available?
- Is the next step understandable and the effort for parents proportionate?
- Does the work respect families and allow different paces and approaches?
- Does every product or comparative claim have an appropriate source?
- Do the tone and visual energy suit the situation?
- Are the name, core message, and available assets used consistently?
- Are missing inputs and proposed choices visible to the next person or agent?

If a check fails, revise the work or identify the specific unresolved dependency. Do not fill a gap with an invented fact or an implied brand approval.
