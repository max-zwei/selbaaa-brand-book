# Child personas — five different ways into the day

- **Audience:** Ages 2–6; named characters aged 3–6. Siblings aged 0–7 are context. No standalone age-2 interview character.
- **Research context:** Everyday participation [S09](../research.md#s09); individual support needs [S10](../research.md#s10); peers [S11](../research.md#s11); pretend play [S12](../research.md#s12); varied interests [S13](../research.md#s13); school transition [S14](../research.md#s14).
- **Boundary:** These sources inform possibilities; they do not prove the specific combinations below or any selba benefit.

## Emil

- **Recognize him:** Carries a shopping bag three steps, hands it back, and still insists that he is helping.
- **Household:** F01 · 3 · Leipzig · Julia, 34, and Tom, 36 · baby sister Leni, 8 months.
- **Everyday setting:** Kindergarten; familiar adults and a small circle of children he recognizes. No invented institution name.

### His day and his world

- Notices a delivery van before noticing that the family has reached the shop.
- Chooses the socks with the vehicles; puts a foot in, gets the heel wrong, and asks Julia to start again.
- Wants to carry a napkin to Leni; later wants Julia's lap while she is holding the baby.
- At kindergarten, brings another child an object as a way into play; sometimes plays beside them instead.
- On Saturday, a low wall or passing street-cleaning vehicle can become the main event.
- A good afternoon: Tom accepts repeated “deliveries” and lets Emil decide which doorbell sound to make.

### Wants, pleasures, and contradictions

- Wants to push the button himself; may ask the adult to lift him rather than abandon the role.
- Finds deliberate silly mistakes funny: the adult trying to put a sock on a hand.
- Likes making Leni laugh; dislikes every adult turning to Leni when he has something to show.
- Can insist “selber” and ask to be carried minutes later; neither cancels the other.
- Does not need a new object to begin a game, but can urgently want the bright vehicle another child is holding.

### Attempts, support, and changing conditions

- **Attempts:** Carries light things, pours a little water with an adult nearby, places his own belongings in a known spot.
- **Requests help:** Asks “Mach auf” when a lid resists; wants the object back after the adult starts it.
- **Workaround:** Uses a chair as a pretend van and delivers the same cushion repeatedly.
- **Abandons:** A fastening after repeated difficulty; returns to it more willingly when not being hurried.
- **Changes with context:** More words and initiative with familiar people; may give one word or no answer to a new adult.
- **Boundary:** Attention, frustration, and physical ability vary by moment; no exact attention-span claim.

### Voice and brand lens

- **Speech:** Concrete phrases and short sentences; can tell a simple familiar event. Not constant baby talk or compulsory mispronunciation.
- **Illustrative speech:** “Ich trag das. Jetzt du.” / “Nur aufmachen. Ich mach weiter.”
- **Shows instead of explains:** Pushes, points, repeats, or changes the activity; a “yes” does not prove understanding.
- **Hypothesis for selba:** Make the child's next action visible and preserve their turn when an adult helps.
- **Misfit:** Long explanations, remote benefits, or treating a request for help as failure.

## Lea

- **Recognize her:** Invites Finn to her café, then objects when he orders something she did not put on the menu.
- **Household:** F02 · 4 · Graz · Sabine, 37, and Jan, 38 · brother Finn, 7.
- **Everyday setting:** Kindergarten; likes a familiar play partner but can disagree about who gets which role.

### Her day and her world

- Picks an outfit by a color she wants today; changes her mind after seeing Finn ready to go.
- Turns grocery unpacking into shopkeeping; knows where the coffee goes because Sabine always looks for it.
- Draws marks on a café menu and tells the adult what they say; does not need conventional reading to run the game.
- Wants Finn to include her in his games; dislikes being permanently assigned the youngest role.
- On Saturday, enjoys choosing something at the market more than being told the whole outing plan.
- A good afternoon: a friend arrives, they invent an animal café, and an adult agrees to be a customer.

### Wants, pleasures, and contradictions

- Likes glitter, bright plastic accessories, and a worn fabric apron; no universal “natural materials” preference.
- Wants her own arrangement of cups left intact; freely borrows a cushion from someone else's room.
- Enjoys being funny by serving an absurd pretend meal and waiting for the adult's reaction.
- Wants her ideas accepted; sometimes leaves a game when another child proposes a better rule.
- Calls Finn unfair, then copies a phrase he used five minutes earlier.

### Attempts, support, and changing conditions

- **Attempts:** Arranges places at the table, rinses vegetables, negotiates who will play which part.
- **Requests help:** Wants an adult nearby when entering a new group; may ask Sabine to explain a disagreement.
- **Workaround:** Moves a game to the balcony when Finn will not leave the living-room arrangement alone.
- **Abandons:** A craft whose result must match an adult example; starts her own version instead.
- **Changes with context:** Talks readily in her own game; can become quiet when adults praise her publicly or ask her to perform.
- **Boundary:** Her assertiveness is situational; do not make every answer loud, oppositional, or dramatic.

### Voice and brand lens

- **Speech:** Conversational sentences, reasons from the immediate situation, occasional long additions; can ask a precise follow-up.
- **Illustrative speech:** “Du darfst Gast sein. Aber heute gibt's keine Pizza.” / “Ich hab das so hingelegt.”
- **Shows instead of explains:** Reassigns a role, rearranges pieces, or demonstrates the rule she means.
- **Hypothesis for selba:** Leave room for choosing and interpreting; recognize the child's arrangement before instructing them to change it.
- **Misfit:** Calling an adult's predetermined sequence “your choice” when there is no meaningful choice.

## Mila

- **Recognize her:** Calls a stream her research station; wants to test one more leaf when Tobias starts putting the snacks away.
- **Household:** F03 · 5 · Winterthur · Tobias, 41, and Anja, 39 · brother Matti, 2.
- **Everyday setting:** Kindergarten; knows some days involve a different collecting adult; does not manage the shift calendar herself.

### Her day and her world

- Asks who will collect her; accepts a change more easily when the adult tells her what will happen next.
- Notices which objects float and invents explanations that do not always survive her next test.
- Makes a home for a toy animal, then wants it protected while Matti explores nearby.
- Likes showing a friend something she discovered; dislikes the friend calling it theirs.
- On a walk, remembers a turning by a tree or building; cannot be assumed to navigate independently.
- A good weekend: everyone follows her suggestion for part of the outing, without turning it into a demonstration of what she has learned.

### Wants, pleasures, and contradictions

- Wants a real task in the kitchen, not an adult redoing everything immediately behind her.
- Laughs at a ridiculous explanation, then tests whether it could somehow be true.
- Wants Matti to join her game when she has a role for him; wants her unfinished setup protected at other times.
- Likes being treated as older; still brings an object to an adult just to be looked at together.
- Can enjoy a familiar game repeatedly rather than always seeking a harder one.

### Attempts, support, and changing conditions

- **Attempts:** Suggests a route, prepares part of a meal, builds and revises an experiment.
- **Requests help:** Wants Anja or Tobias to hold something steady; asks an adult to protect a project from Matti.
- **Workaround:** Moves unfinished pieces to the agreed shelf and draws a sign for the adults.
- **Abandons:** An explanation after deciding to show what happened instead; a project can also simply lose her interest.
- **Changes with context:** After a long kindergarten day, asks for help with something she managed alone the previous morning.
- **Boundary:** Curiosity does not imply exceptional ability, a diagnosis, or a fixed future interest in science.

### Voice and brand lens

- **Speech:** Explains a small sequence, asks “what if,” changes her answer after trying; still uses incomplete causal explanations.
- **Illustrative speech:** “Der schwimmt noch. Warte.” / “Du hältst das fest und ich mache den Rest.”
- **Shows instead of explains:** Repeats a test; a different outcome can matter more than the adult's label.
- **Hypothesis for selba:** Make trying again and asking for a small amount of help possible; allow an unfinished state.
- **Misfit:** Turning every attempt into a lesson, score, or adult-approved result.

## Sami

- **Recognize him:** Talks through the whole bus journey, then watches a new group for several minutes before joining a familiar activity.
- **Household:** F04 · 4 · Dortmund · Aylin, 32 · only child; grandmother helps on one agreed afternoon.
- **Everyday setting:** Kindergarten; no second-parent arrangement, language background, or religion inferred from his name.

### His day and his world

- Watches which bus arrives and points out a vehicle he recognizes; the adult remains responsible for the route.
- Lines up vehicles in a garage, then turns the same arrangement into a rescue scene.
- Brings a familiar toy to begin play with someone he knows; does not always want to share the central vehicle.
- Chooses a vegetable at the shop and may still dislike it prepared a different way.
- At the library, asks for a familiar story before considering a new one.
- A good Saturday: market, playground, and time to repeat a game with a friend rather than move to the next attraction.

### Wants, pleasures, and contradictions

- Wants Aylin to understand where each vehicle belongs; accepts a new arrangement when it is his idea.
- Enjoys a bright, noisy rescue toy as well as quiet matching games; no consistent low-stimulation identity.
- Makes jokes with familiar people; does not owe a new adult an entertaining answer.
- Wants a friend to join; sometimes needs help making the first invitation.
- Likes helping match socks but may decline the same task when he is building something.

### Attempts, support, and changing conditions

- **Attempts:** Matches belongings, fetches familiar items, begins a game with a known child.
- **Requests help:** Wants Aylin beside him for the first exchange in a new group; asks for a difficult closure to be started.
- **Workaround:** Uses the edge of the rug to mark his garage; follows a remembered arrangement rather than written labels.
- **Abandons:** A new game when the rules arrive faster than he can try them; may return after watching someone play.
- **Changes with context:** Will talk at length about a rescue scene at home; may answer an interviewer with a gesture.
- **Boundary:** Do not label this as anxiety, a disorder, or proof he dislikes people.

### Voice and brand lens

- **Speech:** Clear everyday sentences; specific about his interest; fewer words when unfamiliar with the person or task.
- **Illustrative speech:** “Der muss hier parken. Da ist die Garage.” / “Kommst du erst mal mit?”
- **Shows instead of explains:** Moves a piece into place or watches another person before trying.
- **Hypothesis for selba:** Make the first action easy to see; allow watching, repetition, and a nearby adult without commentary about confidence.
- **Misfit:** Assuming every child will immediately join a performance, group challenge, or enthusiastic demonstration.

## Nora

- **Recognize her:** Packs a notebook herself and insists on having that same notebook in both homes; another blank one is not interchangeable to her.
- **Household:** F05 · 6 · Linz · Daniel, 38, and Eva, 37, separated; weekly shared care; only child; first school year.
- **Everyday setting:** School and agreed afternoon care on workdays; adults coordinate directly rather than using Nora as a messenger.

### Her day and her world

- Wants to remember what she needs for school; appreciates a quiet adult check more than a public correction.
- Writes short messages with the letters she knows; asks for help spelling rather than pretending to know every word.
- Draws maps of familiar rooms and routes; mixes actual features with additions for a game.
- Enjoys time with a friend without an adult directing every turn.
- Wants to learn to stop confidently on rollerskates; asks Daniel to stay close on an unfamiliar surface.
- A good weekend: pancakes, a friend visiting, and enough unplanned time to finish the game they started.

### Wants, pleasures, and contradictions

- Wants responsibility for her things; can still forget one and need the adult to help retrieve it.
- Likes some differences between her homes; wants certain belongings and relationships to remain continuous.
- Can explain why a rule seems unfair; does not reason about every situation consistently.
- Enjoys correcting a silly map Daniel draws; dislikes him correcting her own drawing without asking.
- Wants grown-up recognition without every activity becoming “school practice.”

### Attempts, support, and changing conditions

- **Attempts:** Packs a few familiar belongings, helps plan a small outing, invites a friend, writes a message.
- **Requests help:** Wants someone nearby for a new physical task or an unfamiliar social situation.
- **Workaround:** Keeps ordinary basics in both homes; carries the notebook and a few chosen objects.
- **Abandons:** A complicated plan when playing with a friend sounds better; an adult's planned outing is not automatically her priority.
- **Changes with context:** After a demanding school day, may want the adult to do something she usually does herself.
- **Boundary:** Two homes do not automatically mean distress, conflict, or a need for a corrective product.

### Voice and brand lens

- **Speech:** Connected explanations, comparisons, questions about rules; can say she does not know or does not want to discuss something.
- **Illustrative speech:** “Ich brauche nicht irgendein Heft. Da ist meine Karte drin.” / “Bleib hier, bis ich bremsen kann.”
- **Shows instead of explains:** Opens the notebook to the relevant page; distinguishes ownership from an object's basic function.
- **Hypothesis for selba:** Respect ownership, portability, and chosen responsibility; keep help available without babyish framing.
- **Misfit:** Assuming one home, fluent reading, adult purchasing judgment, or independence in every setting.

## Across all five

- Ability on one day is not a promise for the next; help, tiredness, interest, and familiarity change participation.
- A child's perspective can differ from the parent's interpretation without either being made dishonest.
- Enjoyment, refusal, indifference, repetition, and changing one's mind are all valid reactions.
- Never convert a child's “yes,” play, or simulated enthusiasm into purchase intent, consent for unrelated activity, or developmental evidence.
- No child knows selba's strategy, a product's price, or an unseen concept unless the interviewer has supplied it in a form the child could understand.
- Ages guide the fictional voice; actual children vary in language, motor skills, sensory preferences, and support needs.
