## In one minute

| Audience | Recognize them through this tension | What selba should make room for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parents and caregivers | Wants the child to pour the water; reaches for the jug when guests arrive and the table is still unset. Wants a life beyond organizing everyone else's day; also enjoys the child's elaborate explanation of a cardboard ticket machine. | Participation that fits real life; useful help without another parenting assignment; pleasure, experimentation, and different ways of being a family |
| Children | Wants to carry the shopping bag; gives it back when it gets heavy. Announces a café, assigns the adult a seat, and becomes annoyed when the adult starts directing the game. | A real part to play; recognizable invitations; ownership of the attempt; help without losing the role |

- **Brand decision:** German-speaking families in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland; children aged **2–6**.
- **Brand decision:** Named child personas aged **3–6**; age 2 remains in the audience but has no separate interview character. Siblings aged 0–7 provide context, not additional targeting.
- **Brand decision:** Understand the whole family day: work, care, friends, play, meals, errands, hobbies, downtime, and weekends.
- **Brand decision:** €20–40 is the founder's typical intended product range, not a validated willingness-to-pay result or a published price for every product.
- **Hypothesis:** Strongest fit where a caregiver wants to leave a meaningful part to the child, and a simple tool earns its place against doing nothing, using something already owned, borrowing, or making it themselves.
- **No audience filter:** Parent gender, job title, home ownership, partnered status, fixed income, toy brand ownership, or one parenting philosophy.

## Recognition cues beyond demographics

**Hypotheses for brand planning — examples are fictional.**

| Instead of | Use a concrete tension | Implication to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Busy parent | Has time to walk to the shop on Saturday; on Tuesday needs the shopping done between pickup and an appointment | Show how participation can adapt to the moment |
| Wants independence | Lets the child spread the butter; takes over the lid; gives the knife back for the next slice | Preserve a child's part when help is needed |
| Values quality | Checks whether the loose piece can be replaced because the last set became unusable after one piece disappeared | Explain maintenance and contents using verified facts |
| Price-sensitive | Can spend €30, but has already promised a birthday gift and delays an unproven household purchase | Distinguish cash availability from priority and confidence |
| Creative child | Turns the delivery box into a bus and rejects the adult's suggestion to make it a house | Leave room for an interpretation the adult did not plan |
| Social child | Has one friend they will ask to play; watches an unfamiliar group before approaching | Do not turn a situational behavior into a permanent personality label |

## Where the brand fits — and where to leave space

- **Brand decision:** Help children participate; do not define success as speed, obedience, early milestones, or managing alone.
- **Brand decision:** Parents can enjoy their children and want uninterrupted adult time; neither requires guilt-based messaging.
- **Hypothesis:** A visible use is more useful than an abstract developmental promise; validate against actual choices.
- **Hypothesis:** The purchase must justify space, setup, upkeep, and family attention as well as money.
- **Leave space:** Friendships, meals, hobbies, imaginative play, and household differences do not automatically need a selba product.
- **Poor fit for the promise:** Guaranteed compliance, guaranteed developmental gains, or a tool expected to replace adult care.
- **Valid outcome:** A family likes the brand and has no reason to buy the current product.
