## The income question

- **Finding:** None of the retained evidence establishes a minimum gross household income for considering a €20–40 selba product.
- **Finding:** [S04](#s04) reports leisure/culture spending across income bands; this does not show that every household can comfortably fund an additional purchase.
- **Finding:** [S05](#s05), [S06](#s06), [S07](#s07), and [S08](#s08) use household-type, composition, income, or expenditure measures; these are not interchangeable with discretionary budgets.
- **Decision:** Remove explicit income, fixed parent ages as eligibility criteria, and automatic price brackets such as “under €30 is easy.”
- **Decision:** Keep exact income unspecified in fictional profiles; describe purchasing flexibility through concrete tradeoffs.
- **Decision:** Do not convert the founder's euro range into an assumed Swiss retail price or willingness to pay.

| Dimension | Useful evidence from a real family | Unsupported shortcut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordability | What an additional purchase would displace this month | Salary or postcode alone |
| Willingness to pay | Recent purchase, paid price, reason, and rejected alternative | “They said they liked the idea” |
| Value | Which existing action or object the product would replace or improve | Attractive materials imply usefulness |
| Timing | Immediate need, planned replacement, gift, or no current occasion | Every interested family is ready now |
| Effort | Who sets up, stores, cleans, repairs, and explains it | Low purchase price implies low burden |
| Repeat use | Actual use after novelty, loss of a piece, or a change of routine | Simulated enthusiasm predicts retention |

## DACH interpretation

| Market | Evidence used | Application | Do not infer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Germany | DJI care analysis; Destatis expenditure tables; miniKIM | Different work/care patterns and budget tradeoffs; mixed media attitudes | Leipzig or Dortmund represents Germany; all customers match a survey's main caregiver |
| Austria | ÖIF mental-work research; Statistik Austria household income and consumption | Separate coordination from execution; account for household composition | Graz and Linz personalities follow national traits; Austrian and German gross incomes mean the same thing |
| German-speaking Switzerland | BFS family finance context; Swiss Family Barometer | Consider recurring costs and family arrangements; retain local context | Whole-Switzerland findings equal German-speaking preschool-family findings; EUR and CHF prices are interchangeable |

- **Fictional persona detail:** Actual work hours, pickup times, family arrangements, and local terminology in the cast describe those households only.
- **Limit:** Child-development guidance is predominantly German; no DACH-wide child-behavior prevalence estimate is supplied.
- **Limit:** Qualitative accounts are weighted toward internet-using parents willing to post publicly; Austrian first-person coverage is thin.

## What changed — and what remains unproven

| Previous assumption | Revision | Still requires real evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affluent parents defined by €80k+ | Behavior and purchasing circumstances; no income cutoff | Who actually buys at the proposed price, and what they forgo |
| 80% mothers / 20% fathers | No asserted buyer split; show several care arrangements | Who discovers, evaluates, pays, uses, and recommends |
| One narrow routine and familiar devices explain the audience | Whole days, relationships, interests, small pleasures, and contextual friction | Which situations selba can usefully address |
| Rich biographies imply research validity | Explicit fiction labels, source-linked patterns, concise profiles | Whether real families recognize these combinations |
| All three children have similar interests | Five children, including two four-year-olds with different preferences | Missing or overrepresented perspectives |
| Interview agents claim to be real participants | Clearly identified simulations | Real usability, demand, outcomes, and lived experience |
