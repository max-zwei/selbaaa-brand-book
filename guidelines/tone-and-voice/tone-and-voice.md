# selba tone & voice

**Last update**: 30 October 2026
**Owner:** Max Pinkert, max@selbaaaa.com
**v1.1:** Holistic DACH audience and five-family references

For people writing for selba and agents helping them. Instructions and explanations are in English; original copy examples are in German. This is an editorial system informed by research, not a scientifically validated communication intervention.

## Start here

**selba notices the small thing, makes the next step understandable, and leaves the family in charge.**

At a crowded kitchen table, selba is the person who listens long enough to understand what is actually being asked. They can explain why something works without turning the answer into a lesson. They do not turn the child into the problem or the parent into a project.

Our language should give people something they can use, a little room to decide, and occasionally something to smile at. The combination is the voice. No individual word, joke, or sentence pattern has to appear in every message.

| Rule | Do this on the page | Check before finishing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **V1 — Notice before interpreting.** | Name the actual situation or question. Use one telling detail when useful. | Did we describe what we know, or invent how this person feels? |
| **V2 — Put the useful bit first.** | Give the answer, practical distinction, or next action before the brand story. | Can a busy reader find what they came for? |
| **V3 — Leave the steering with the family.** | Offer a real choice, explain a constraint, and allow a product not to fit. | Does this sentence make someone prove they are a good parent? |
| **V4 — Make knowledge inspectable.** | Explain the mechanism or evidence at the level the question needs. Name the limit. | Are we confusing an intention, an observation, and a demonstrated result? |
| **V5 — Let one small thing be funny.** | Use a brief observation about a situation, object, or our own assumption. | Would this still be respectful if the reader did not laugh? |
| **V6 — Notice what the child does.** | Describe trying, choosing, asking, discovering, or accepting help. | Did we turn independence into a score, deadline, or duty to make adults' lives easier? |

**Default delivery:** German, lowercase *du* for one person and *ihr* for a family or group; natural inclusive wording; concrete verbs; complete, varied sentences. Answer more directly when someone is uncertain. Humor is optional and disappears in complaints, safety concerns, and distress.

**Avoid:** parental guilt as a sales argument; invented reassurance; developmental guarantees; automatic jokes; baby talk; declarations of scientific authority without usable evidence; claims that selba has already checked, refunded, shipped, or solved something when it has not.

**Before publishing:** check facts, audience, useful answer, agency, dignity, and tone. A plain service answer can be entirely on-brand. Do not add a signature flourish to prove that it is selba.

## 1. Who is speaking, and to whom?

### Our relationship with parents

selba is a capable, observant companion in the practical work of family life. We are not the family therapist, the school report, the parenting influencer, or the friend who claims to know everything that happened today. We can know our product well without claiming to know someone else's child.

Parents are the primary audience. Write for adults who can make judgments and may have very little time for us. Some want an explanation; others need the answer in one sentence. Time pressure is a possibility to accommodate, not an identity to assign to every parent.

The supplied [parent personas](../../personas/parents/parents.md) give five editorial lenses across ordinary family life. Use the [audience overview](../../personas/README.md) for household facts and the [research register](../../personas/research.md) for evidence and limits.

| Lens | What to make available | What not to copy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Julia | A visible use and realistic effort; room for personal ambitions as well as family life | Presumed guilt, compulsory marketing jargon, or a permanently overwhelmed identity |
| Sabine | Specific use, upkeep, and room to adapt; respect for existing workarounds | Clinical explanations, moral judgments about materials, or a rigid “less stuff” doctrine |
| Tobias | Understandable handovers, flexibility, and enough information to judge fit | Treating children as processes, every outing as an achievement, or adult plans as the only good outcome |
| Aylin | The full commitment, including setup and total cost; respect for personal priorities | Pity, “only €30,” or assuming another adult is always available |
| Daniel | Language that works across households; responsibility with support available | Assuming one home, parental conflict, or a need to compensate with purchases |

These are fictional profiles, not interview findings or measured segments. Research supports identified patterns within its stated limits; biographies, quotations, and simulated reactions remain invented. The audience description does not justify assuming every reader is a mother, partnered, affluent, neurotypical, or raising a child on a standard developmental timetable.

### Children's place in the voice

Children are central to what we make and how we speak about it. They are rarely the readers of the brand's adult-facing communication. Do not use a child's speaking style to address their parent.

The [child personas](../../personas/children/children.md), aged 3–6 within the broader 2–6 audience, explore play, friendship, ownership, participation, and choosing help across everyday situations. Treat that as a design prompt, not a universal developmental finding. Direct child-facing language should describe something visible, offer a manageable invitation, and make help available. It does not need exaggerated excitement. Spoken examples must also make sense without written emphasis or punctuation.

Independence includes choosing help. A child who does something with support has not failed the brand promise.

### What the original direction becomes

| Starting input | Decision for this foundation |
| --- | --- |
| Quiet party guest who listens and eases tension | Keep. Show listening by answering the specific question, not by repeatedly announcing that we understand. |
| Educational background that stays in the background | Keep. Let the quality and limits of the explanation establish competence. |
| “Caring & Patient” | Operationalize as attention to the actual situation, unhurried choices, and no demand for quick progress. |
| “Trust” and research-backed guidance | Replace a general aura of science with traceable, appropriately scoped claims. |
| Lean towards funny | Revise to humor available, never owed. A reader should not need to signal distress before we become useful. |
| Enthusiasm for children; solid foundation for parents | Use audience-appropriate vocabulary and pace, with dignity and accuracy unchanged. Children for example do not automatically need a louder voice. |
| Do not “educate parents” | Explain the requested thing; avoid grading, correcting, or prescribing the family's entire way of living. |

## 2. The six voice principles

### V1 — Notice before interpreting

Start with the detail the reader supplied, or an explicitly illustrative everyday scene. A missing mitten is more useful than a diagnosis of modern parenthood. In replies, acknowledge what happened; do not infer an entire emotional state from one sentence.

**Writing moves:** use a concrete noun; refer to the actual question; acknowledge a repeated contact or failed step when known. When someone names a feeling, respond to that feeling without escalating it. Do not mechanically restate every message.

**Boundary:** observation must not become surveillance, exaggerated intimacy, or a stock picture of exhausted mothers. Avoid opening every message with a reassurance formula.

**Example — brief:** A parent says the same setup step failed twice. No cause has been established.

> **Off-brand:** Wir wissen genau, wie frustrierend dein chaotischer Familienalltag ist.
>
> **selba:** Die Einrichtung ist zweimal an derselben Stelle abgebrochen. Welche Meldung erscheint dort?

The revision uses the known event and asks for information that can change the next step. It does not claim to know the parent's life. First-person accounts show how much context a snapshot can miss; this rule is our editorial response, not a measured outcome. [research.md/S01] [research.md/S03]

### V2 — Put the useful bit first

Give the answer before the atmosphere. For instructions, name the action and object. For a choice, show the relevant difference. For an article, state what question it will answer. Short is useful only when it remains clear.

**Writing moves:** lead with a direct answer; put conditions beside the claim they qualify; keep one main thought per sentence; move optional detail below the main answer. Use stable names for the same component.

**Boundary:** do not make every sentence a fragment or remove necessary reasons, cautions, and conditions to sound brisk. Brevity must not become brusqueness.

**Example — hypothetical facts:** A card set has six cards that users can arrange freely and needs no app.

> **Off-brand:** Entdecke eine innovative Lösung, die Familien auf ihrer einzigartigen Reise begleitet.
>
> **selba:** Sechs Karten, die ihr in eure Reihenfolge legt. Mehr nicht.

The revision tells a parent what the object is and how it is used. Its feature claims are allowed only because this brief supplies them. Clear language and task-first structure are supported as editorial practices by [research.md/S32], [research.md/S33], [research.md/S35], and [research.md/S36].

### V3 — Leave the steering with the family

Explain what is possible and let the reader decide whether it fits. A purchase, a new routine, or an interest in our advice is not proof of love. Avoid permission-giving language that makes the brand the judge of acceptable parenting.

**Writing moves:** describe an option and its conditions; allow “not for us”; distinguish a suggestion from an instruction. Offer one relevant alternative rather than a new optimization project. A real requirement can be stated directly and explained.

**Boundary:** choice is not an excuse to make safety rules optional or to disguise a required action as a question. Do not tell a distressed parent to decide everything alone. Give the help requested within our actual competence.

**Example — brief:** A parent asks whether a new routine tool is necessary when their existing routine works.

> **Off-brand:** Wer sein Kind wirklich fördern möchte, sollte jetzt den nächsten Schritt gehen.
>
> **selba:** Wenn euer Ablauf für euch passt, müsst ihr nichts daran ändern.

This is an answer, not an indirect sales pitch. Our agency rule is consistent with autonomy-supportive communication research, but research on parent-child interactions does not establish sales or wellbeing effects for this brand voice. [research.md/S04] [research.md/S05]

### V4 — Make knowledge inspectable

Replace prestige language with an explanation the reader can examine. Say what a product does, what the design is intended to support, and what has actually been studied. Expertise should make a question easier to judge.

**Writing moves:** use the mechanism before the label; disclose the relevant uncertainty; link a useful source in explanatory content; identify when a finding concerns a different age group, setting, or intervention. Saying “we do not know” can be a complete and competent answer.

**Boundary:** “can,” “may,” and “designed to” do not rescue an unsupported implication. A general study does not validate a selba product. Do not smuggle neuroscience into copy through terms such as *Regulation* or *Nervensystem*.

**Example — hypothetical brief:** Cards display the steps in a routine; no efficacy study has been supplied.

> **Off-brand:** Wissenschaftlich bewiesen: mehr Selbstständigkeit in nur sieben Tagen.
>
> **selba:** Die Karten zeigen die einzelnen Schritte. Ob sie euren Alltag erleichtern, probiert ihr selbst mit unserer Hilfe aus.

This states the feature and separates it from an outcome. Caspers describes first understanding a topic and being honest about not knowing; Nguyen-Kim describes consulting experts and literature despite her training. Their approaches are communication references, not endorsements of selba. [research.md/S06] [research.md/S09]

### V5 — Let one small thing be funny

Use a small, recognizable discrepancy: the plan is ready, the shoe is missing. Keep the observation proportionate. The reader can recognize it without being asked to laugh at a child or partner.

**Writing moves:** one quiet aside can be enough; let the last concrete detail carry the humor; avoid explaining the joke. In a short piece, default to no more than one comic beat. Longer writing can use more when the brief warrants it; this is an editorial default, not a quota.

**Boundary:** no humor about distress, developmental differences, money worries, injuries, or a customer's service failure. Do not imitate a person's catchphrases. Sarcasm directed at the reader is out. Humiliation does not become acceptable because the delivery is understated.

**Example — illustrative social caption, not a customer reply:**

> **Off-brand:** Kleine Tyrannen, große Dramen 😂 Wer kennt's nicht?!
>
> **selba:** Jacke an. Schuhe an. Zweiten Schuh suchen. Ersten Schuh ausziehen. Zweiten Schuh anziehen. Ersten Schuh anziehen.

The object and sequence carry the joke. The child is not its target. This is a choice informed by Chilli's official characterization and the contrast between understated humor and humor that obstructs service copy. It is not a claim that all parents share this taste. [research.md/S13] [research.md/S27] [research.md/S29] [research.md/S32]

### V6 — Notice what the child does

Describe the action rather than declaring the child advanced, compliant, or exceptional. Celebrate a discovery without turning it into a milestone competition. The child remains a person with their own interest in the activity.

**Writing moves:** use verbs such as *probieren*, *wählen*, *zeigen*, *fragen*, and *entdecken* when they describe something real. Mention help without framing it as a lesser result. Address a child directly only when the content is actually for them.

**Boundary:** no “big children do this,” no comparison with siblings, no “finally,” and no suggestion that autonomy means being left alone. Keep safety and supervision requirements intact. Specific praise can be appropriate; constant evaluation is unnecessary.

**Example — hypothetical observed moment:** A child has chosen the order of three pictures.

> **Off-brand:** Wow, du bist schon viel weiter als andere Kinder!
>
> **selba:** Du hast eine Reihenfolge für dich gefunden, welches Bild zuerst kommt, sehr gut!

The child owns the choice without being ranked. The official *Baby Race* synopsis provides a fictional counterexample to milestone comparison; Caspers explicitly rejects condescending child-directed presentation. [research.md/S14] [research.md/S07]

## 3. One voice, different situations

Adjust tone to the task and the reader's expressed state. Do not infer emotions from demographic labels. The stable elements are attention, useful detail, agency, factual honesty, and the child's dignity.

| Situation | Opening and detail | Humor and pace | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery / acquisition | Name a recognizable activity and explain the offer or intent | One optional observation; room for curiosity | Inventing a deficiency to create demand |
| Product explanation | What it is, how it works, what it requires | Plain sequence; humor usually unnecessary | Benefits that quietly exceed the feature |
| Purchase hesitation | Answer the actual objection; make fit and limits visible | Unhurried; no push to close | Guilt, false urgency, defending the price with parental love |
| Delight or a shared discovery | Respond to the specific thing the person shares | A warmer rhythm; one exclamation can fit | Claiming the product caused the child's progress |
| Frustration / complaint | Name the failure, own our part where known, offer the available next action | No jokes, emojis, or cheerful opener | “No worries,” a brand story, or blame disguised as advice |
| Uncertainty / evidence question | State what is known and what is not; explain only the relevant distinction | Patient and direct; no humor by default | Defending authority or drowning the answer in citations |
| Guilt, distress, sensitive concern | Respond to the stated concern within our role; no sales turn | Steady, literal, no humor | Diagnosing, minimizing, or certifying someone as a good parent |
| Direct invitation to a child | One visible action or genuine choice; simple spoken syntax | Interested, not artificially excited | Baby talk, performance pressure, hidden obligation |

The same hypothetical feature can be expressed differently without changing its facts:

- **Discovery:** „Sechs Bilder für euren Ablauf. Welche Reihenfolge passt zu euch?“
- **Explanation:** „Lege die sechs Karten in die Reihenfolge, in der ihr sie nutzen möchtet.“
- **Uncertainty:** „Ihr legt die Reihenfolge selbst fest. Ob das für euch hilfreich ist, seht ihr im ersten Ablauf.“

No version promises a calmer evening. In the second version the person has already chosen to use the product, so a clear imperative is appropriate.

## 4. German language conventions

These are selba's house decisions, not universal rules for German brands.

| Element | Default | Example or boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address | Lowercase *du, dir, dein* for an individual; *ihr, euch, euer* for a family or group | „Was möchtest du wissen?“ / „Welche Reihenfolge passt zu euch?“ Do not switch the referent mid-sentence. |
| Formal relationships | Keep *Sie, Ihnen, Ihr* when the existing exchange is formal or the brief requires it | A retailer's formal inquiry does not need forced familiarity. |
| Inclusive language | Prefer natural neutral nouns and direct address | *Eltern, Familien, Bezugspersonen, Fachkräfte, Team*. Use a specific relationship only when known and relevant. |
| Family roles | Do not assign care, skill, purchasing, or absence by gender | Avoid the competent mother / helpless father joke. Do not assume two adults are available. |
| Brand name | *selba*, lowercase, including in headlines and body | Product names follow their approved spelling. Respell "selber" always to "selba". |
| Rhythm | Usually one main thought per sentence; mix short and medium sentences | Flag sentences over about 25 words for review, not automatic rejection. Avoid a whole page of two-word fragments. |
| Vocabulary | Familiar, concrete verbs and nouns | Prefer describing the actual action to *Potenzialentfaltung*, *Empowerment*, or *ganzheitliche Förderung*. |
| Technical language | Use it when necessary, then explain it | Do not replace a precise term with a misleading simplification. A persona's vocabulary is not a writing requirement. |
| Anglicisms | Keep established terms when they help; avoid imported marketing emphasis | *App* and *Newsletter* can be useful. *Gamechanger*, *Parenting-Hack*, and *Must-have* usually add no information. |
| Questions | Ask only a real question whose answer matters | Avoid „Willst du nicht auch das Beste für dein Kind?“ No automatic question at the end of every reply. |
| Calls to action | State the actual action or destination | „So funktioniert es“, „Inhalt ansehen“, „Frage stellen“. Use only when the destination exists. |
| Punctuation | Normal German spelling and sentence case; German quotation marks in prose | No repeated exclamation or question marks. Dashes and parentheses are occasional tools, not a rhythm template. |
| Exclamations | Usually zero in service and explanation; one can suit a genuine celebration | Excitement must come from the event, not punctuation volume. |
| Emojis | None by default; an optional, intelligible emoji in an informal social context | Never carry essential meaning; none for complaints, distress, evidence, or safety. |
| Diminutives and labels | Use only literal, contextually appropriate terms | Avoid routine *Mäuschen*, *kleine Helden*, *Mini-Genies*, *Tyrannen*, *Supermamas*. |
| “We” | selba's actual team or authorized organizational voice | No invented „Wir Eltern kennen das …“ or fabricated personal experience. |
| Praise | Specific and proportionate | „Du hast einen anderen Weg gefunden.“ Avoid global rankings or praise for suppressing needs. |
| Plain language | Accessible standard German | Do not claim compliance with a formal *Leichte Sprache* standard or certification. |

### The selbaaaaa signature

The supplied core message is „Guck mal, ich kann das schon selbaaaaa.“ Preserve that exact approved line when using it as a brand signature. Its function is to evoke a child's self-initiated moment of pride, not to set an expectation for when a child should manage alone.

Use the signature sparingly in brand storytelling or a suitable celebratory context. Do not append it to replies, instructions, apologies, or evidence discussions. Do not turn it into a general license for baby spelling, extended vowels, or a fake testimonial. A portrayed child voice must be clearly illustrative unless it is a real, authorized quotation. The broader message must also leave room for „Hilfst du mir?“

### Words that require a second look

These are review signals, not a blanket ban on discussing the words themselves:

- *einfach, nur, schnell*: Do they describe a verified feature, or make someone's difficulty sound trivial?
- *endlich, schon, noch nicht*: Is a developmental timetable being imposed? The fixed signature above is a narrow contextual exception, not permission for comparative progress copy.
- *wissenschaftlich, bewiesen, fördert, reguliert, beruhigt*: Which exact evidence supports which exact outcome?
- *perfekt, garantiert, immer, jedes Kind, die einzige*: Is an absolute claim both necessary and supportable?
- *du darfst, du musst nur*: Are we appointing ourselves as the parent's permission-giver or oversimplifying the situation?
- *wertvoll, achtsam, liebevoll, magisch*: Can a concrete detail do more work than the adjective?

## 5. Facts, promises, and sensitive boundaries

### Separate four kinds of statement

| Kind | What supports it | How to express it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product fact | Current approved specification or explicitly supplied verified brief | State it precisely, including material conditions. |
| Design intention | Documented design rationale | Identify it as the purpose of a design choice; do not present the intended result as achieved. |
| General research finding | An identifiable source with a relevant method, population, and result | Explain what was studied and the limit of transfer. |
| Product outcome | Evidence about the actual product and the outcome claimed | Match strength, population, conditions, and scope to that evidence. If absent, do not claim it. |

The studies in this guide inform communication choices. They do not establish that selba improves cognition, sleep, independence, attachment, or family wellbeing. The toddler study is exploratory and based on parent reports; the psychological-control study concerns older children. Neither tests brand copy. [research.md/S04] [research.md/S05]

For a consequential claim, the working brief should identify the source, relevant population, what was measured, and what the evidence does **not** establish. Consumer copy does not need a citation after every ordinary fact; it does need support available behind the claim. Evidence-focused answers should link the actual source where useful. Never invent a citation or imply that a study was reviewed when only a headline was available.

### Translate the brand's tensions deliberately

| Tension | Public-language decision |
| --- | --- |
| Challenging attention-seeking products while avoiding parental guilt | Describe a verified feature, such as the absence of a screen, if true. Do not infer that other choices damage children or that selba redeems the parent. |
| Scientific ambition without a professor's voice | Explain the particular design decision and evidence limit. Credentials are not a substitute for an answer. |
| Easier family life and child-led exploration | Describe what an adult no longer needs to do only when supported; never promise a compliant or conveniently silent child. |
| Humor and reassurance | Answer the concern first. If a joke would require a generous reader to feel safe, leave it out. |
| Independence and care | Present trying, pausing, and asking for help as compatible. Do not sell withdrawal of adult attention. |
| “The only” positioning | Keep it as an internal strategic ambition unless a specific public comparison is substantiated and kept current. |

For ordinary wellbeing questions, stay within the requested product or communication role. Do not diagnose a child, interpret attachment, or prescribe treatment. If a message describes immediate danger or injury, prioritize clear, appropriate safety guidance and human escalation under the actual service process; stop selling and joking. Never invent a safety procedure, certification, recall, or guarantee.

## 6. Replies and conversation

Compose a response around the person's need, not a fixed empathy script. Use only the parts required:

1. Answer the question, or identify what prevents a reliable answer.
2. Acknowledge the specific event or concern when that adds something.
3. Give an available action, useful distinction, or actual choice.
4. Ask the smallest question that changes what happens next. Then stop.

For a complaint, acknowledge a known failure and our responsibility without a defensive explanation. Apologize for the event, not for the person's reaction. Request only information needed to help. Move order details to an appropriate private channel; do not request them in a public comment.

**Never claim completed work from a draft.** „Wir haben dir den Betrag erstattet“ requires confirmation that the refund happened. „Wir erstatten dir den Betrag“ requires an authorized decision and process. Otherwise, ask the operator for the missing fact or use a clearly marked draft placeholder; do not substitute a reassuring fiction.

Mirror the established level of formality, not the customer's aggression, contempt, or child-directed labels. If someone calls their child difficult, respond to the situation without adopting that label. A parent's self-deprecating joke is not permission for the brand to make the same joke about them.

Avoid repetitive openings such as „Danke für deine ehrliche Frage“, „Wir verstehen dich total“, or „Das ist völlig normal“. They can be legitimate in context, but they must earn their place and may require facts we do not have.

## 7. How future sub-voices inherit this foundation

The foundation applies across products, channels, and stages of the brand. A sub-voice may change expression; it may not silently change the relationship with families or the standard of evidence.

**Fixed:** V1–V6; dignity; factual honesty; no guilt-based persuasion; no developmental ranking; no invented commitments; German address and inclusion defaults unless the approved context explicitly requires another register or language.

**Adjustable:** length, amount of explanation, rhythm, vocabulary needed for the audience, degree of playfulness, and channel conventions. Humor can always move down to zero. Mandatory product and safety information must remain clear and intact.

| Purpose and channel | What the communication helps someone do |

**Worked hypothetical extension: rainy-day play invitations.** Audience: parents seeking one optional idea; children aged 3–5 hearing a short invitation. Context: a non-product editorial activity with paper, approved separately for its intended use. Adjustments: short, visible action words; a slightly more playful opening. No claim about development, attention span, or time occupied. This is a portability test, not an approved product or activity specification.

> **Parent-facing:** Ein Blatt Papier. Was könnte daraus werden? Ihr könnt etwas falten, zeichnen oder erst einmal Ideen sammeln.
>
> **Child-facing:** Was möchtest du aus dem Papier machen?

**Boundary test:** if the parent says the child does not want to join, answer „Dann passt die Idee gerade nicht. Ihr müsst daraus keine Aufgabe machen.“ Do not add encouragement to persist, a developmental argument, or a purchase suggestion. This extension changes the surface while preserving V3 and V6.

A proposed extension that needs guilt, unsupported promises, or ridicule is a conflict to flag to the brand owner, not a valid exception. Keep a short change log when the foundation changes and rerun the affected examples and benchmarks.

## 8. Editorial review

Use these gates before scoring style:

- **Truth:** Every factual claim and organizational commitment has a source in the actual brief. No fixture facts or old examples have leaked into live copy.
- **Dignity and agency:** No shame, pressure tied to love, child ranking, or ridicule. A choice is real; a necessary instruction is clear.
- **Situation:** Serious concerns receive a direct answer without jokes, emojis, or an upsell.
- **Usability:** The requested information or action is present. Necessary conditions are not buried or removed.

Then check V1–V6 using **0 = violated, 1 = acceptable but generic, 2 = specifically demonstrated, N/A = not needed here**. A ready draft has no violated applicable principle. Give 2 only for an identifiable language choice; absence of a joke can satisfy V5 in a serious response. Do not chase a total score or force all six principles into a button label.

For campaign or editorial copy, ask whether the combination of concrete family detail, restrained observation, and room for the child is visible where appropriate. For service copy, being unmistakably useful matters more than being unmistakably branded.

Read German examples aloud. Remove translated English idioms, awkward nominal phrases, and manufactured intimacy. For spoken child-facing content, check that one hearing is enough to understand the invitation.

## 9. Benchmark set

**18 scenarios: 16 parent-facing, two child-facing.** These are authored training examples, not research quotations or approved product copy. Facts in a scenario belong only to that scenario. They deliberately include long and short formats, ordinary moments, and situations where humor must disappear.

### B01 — Brand introduction · parent

**Brief:** Homepage introduction based on selba's documented intention to develop simple, creative tools for children's participation. No product outcomes established.

**Off-brand:** „Die revolutionäre Kinder-Marke für Eltern, die nur das Beste wollen. Entfalte jetzt das volle Potenzial deines Kindes!“

**selba:** „Platz fürs Selbermachen. Wir entwickeln einfache Ideen für die kleinen Dinge, die Kinder selbst ausprobieren möchten.“

**Why:** V3, V4, V6. Expresses an intention and the child's activity without a developmental result or test of parental ambition. A live product page must add concrete product facts nearby.

### B02 — Product description · parent

**Brief / hypothetical facts:** Six illustrated cards; users arrange their sequence; no app required. No proven outcome.

**Off-brand:** „Die clevere All-in-one-Lösung für stressfreie Routinen und selbstbewusste Kinder.“

**selba:** „Sechs Bildkarten für euren Ablauf. Ihr legt sie in die Reihenfolge, die zu euch passt. Mehr nicht.“

**Why:** V2–V4. Gives contents, use, and a relevant condition. Does not turn the lack of an app into moral superiority.

### B03 — Packaging opener · parent

**Brief / hypothetical facts:** Same six-card set as B02. This is the introductory copy, not a replacement for required labeling.

**Off-brand:** „Mach dein Kind jetzt zum Alltagshelden!“

**selba:** „Was kommt bei euch zuerst? Legt die sechs Karten in eure Reihenfolge.“

**Why:** V2, V3, V6. The question has a real answer and leads directly to use. No claim about the child's status.

### B04 — Social observation · parent

**Brief:** Standalone illustrative caption about leaving the house; no product pitch or implied customer story.

**Off-brand:** „Wenn dein kleiner Chef mal wieder den Familienalltag sabotiert 😂“

**selba:** „Alle fertig zum Losgehen. Bis auf den zweiten Schuh.“

**Why:** V1, V5. The humor sits in an object and a small reversal. It does not appoint a family member as the obstacle.

### B05 — Longer editorial opening · parent

**Brief:** Introduce an article about noticing opportunities to try things independently. No claim that it produces developmental benefits; no product sale.

**Off-brand:** „Jeder Moment ist eine Lernchance. Mit unseren fünf Hacks nutzt du den Alltag optimal und bereitest dein Kind auf eine erfolgreiche Zukunft vor.“

**selba:** „Der Reißverschluss hakt. Dein Kind zieht noch einmal daran. Du hältst die Jacke fest. Selbermachen kann auch so aussehen: jemand probiert, jemand hilft. Es muss nicht gleich alles allein gehen. In diesem Beitrag schauen wir auf solche kleinen Momente im Alltag: Was möchte dein Kind gerade selbst versuchen? Und wo ist Unterstützung gefragt? Daraus muss kein neuer Plan für euren Tag werden.“

**Why:** V1, V2, V3, V6. A concrete scene supports a longer thought; assistance and agency coexist. The final sentence prevents the article from becoming another assignment. This is editorial framing, not a developmental intervention.
