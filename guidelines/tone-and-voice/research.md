## Appendix: Research synthesis and character references

### Method and confidence

Research date: 29 October 2026. The implementation used **23 Exa discovery queries returning 118 result entries** before deduplication. Search hits are not the same as examined sources.

**39 unique source pages were fetched; 36 were retained after reading substantive text or relevant study sections.** The retained sources are indexed in Appendix B. Longer articles and papers were read in relevant extracted sections, not represented as exhaustive full-text or systematic reviews. 

The source set comprises five audience/research sources, seven real-person sources, seven fictional-character sources, twelve brand surfaces, and five editorial sources. It meets the intended breadth, but it does not establish which voice will perform best with selba's actual audience. The brand sample is purposive, the public parent accounts are self-selected, and English-language character techniques require German adaptation.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Empirical:** research with a described method and population; relevance and transfer limits remain explicit.
- **Observed:** wording, scenes described by official sources, or first-hand creator/interview accounts. These establish a technique or portrayal, not its effect on customers.
- **Editorial decision:** selba's choice about how to use, constrain, or reject a technique.

### What changed because of the research

| Finding | Evidence and limit | Resulting decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Practical burdens can be small, cumulative, and invisible to an outsider | First-person accounts [S01–S03]; not a representative survey | V1: one accurate observation instead of a universal statement about parents |
| Avoiding pressure and actively making room for agency are not identical | Empirical distinction in older-child research [S05]; toddler practices in [S04]; neither tests copy | V3: provide real choices and reasons, rather than merely removing harsh words |
| Accessible explanations need not adopt a teacher's social position | Original interviews [S06], [S07], [S09]; communicator practice, not an effectiveness study | V4: show the basis and the limit; keep professional authority out of the spotlight |
| Reassurance can become another ideal to live up to | Nora Imlau's interview [S10] and the fictional milestone comparison in [S14]; different kinds of evidence | Avoid perfect-parent imagery and blanket reassurance; never borrow a fictional parent as a standard customers should meet |
| Useful category copy often describes physical action; less useful copy expands into large outcomes | Twelve sampled surfaces [S20–S31]; observations, not a claim about all competitors | Keep action verbs and distinctions; interrogate abstract growth and transformation claims |
| A recognizable comic voice can interrupt the task | Contact-copy contrast [S27], [S29], [S31]; editorial judgment about selba's needs | V5: a small optional observation; no compulsory performance in service interactions |
| Tone can change without changing the speaking relationship | Practitioner guidance [S32–S35]; cross-language transfer is imperfect | A situational tone matrix and German-native examples, not one adjective slider |

Here, source ranges such as S01–S03 refer to the individually linked entries in Appendix B.

### Six brand comparisons, two surfaces each

These observations concern the sampled text, not company quality, product efficacy, or a complete brand audit. A source's commercial claim is not treated as validated because it appears on an official page.

| Brand and surfaces | Observed language mechanism | selba decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| tonies: product [S20], setup help [S21] | Product copy bundles play, independence, and reassuring outcomes; help switches to named steps and conditions | Borrow the concrete action sequence. Do not import automatic reassurance or sleep/development outcomes. |
| Stapelstein: uses/product page [S22], FAQ [S23] | Broad language about potential sits alongside tangible verbs and material explanations | Prefer the visible actions. Separate educational philosophy from evidence about a product. |
| woom: model guide [S24], size help [S25] | Helps a buyer distinguish size, use, and skill; routes uncertainty to a tool or contact | Borrow fit-based explanation and a relevant next step. Avoid unnecessary superlatives and progress language. |
| innocent: about [S26], contact [S27] | Humorous asides make the organization a character; contact options are wrapped in escalating playful suggestions | Borrow occasional self-directed humor. Reject joke accumulation where the reader needs help. |
| Oatly: product [S28], contact [S29] | Meta-advertising, repetition, and exaggerated self-description invite the reader into a knowing joke | Useful boundary reference: selba can know it is a brand without making the reader process a satire of marketing. |
| IKEA: brand introduction [S30], service [S31] | Broad everyday-life ambition becomes direct verbs and task labels in service | Borrow navigable, practical language. Replace stock reassurance with the actual information when a problem is known. |

This suggests a specific opportunity for selba: **the care of noticing, the competence of explaining, and the restraint to stop after the useful part**. That is our synthesis, not a proven market gap.

### The 12-reference longlist

Six real people and six fictional characters. The selection records useful techniques and misfit, not whole-person judgments. Names are internal reference tools; customer copy should not require recognizing them.

| Reference | Evidence / observable feature | Disposition and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ralph Caspers — real** | Concrete explanation, admitted uncertainty, rejection of condescending presentation [S06] [S07] | **Core anchor:** precision without talking down. Leave behind presenter performance and an obligation to entertain. |
| **Mai Thi Nguyen-Kim — real** | In an original interview, distinguishes training from the ongoing work of consulting experts and reading literature [S09] | **Core anchor:** earned knowledge and intellectual openness. Do not imitate an explainer show's length or import authority by association. |
| **Nora Imlau — real** | Discusses pressure, limited capacity, and the limitations of a simple individual fix [S10] | **Core anchor:** parents' needs belong in the conversation. Do not import clinical-sounding generalizations or make selba an advice authority. |
| Fred Rogers — real | Answers a particular childhood fear with a concrete explanation; declines to generalize about numbers of parents [S08] | Supporting reference for careful attention. Adult copy must not adopt a child's instructional register or his historical parenting claims wholesale. |
| Loriot — real | Describes comedy built from failed communication and misunderstanding [S11] | Boundary reference: notice a small mismatch, but do not reproduce humiliation, domestic contempt, or a deliberately unresolved misunderstanding. |
| Bob Ross — real | Official account presents approachable demonstrations and mistakes as opportunities [S12] | Supporting reference for inviting a first attempt. This is an interested official biography, not a scene transcript; reject reflexive positivity when something has genuinely failed. |
| **Chilli Heeler — fictional** | Official characterization includes wry observation; *Baby Race* shows comparison and uncertainty [S13] [S14] | **Core anchor:** composed without being infallible. Never present idealized parental availability as the expected norm. |
| **Pettersson — fictional** | Nordqvist describes practical chains of obstacles and a relationship combining freedom with security [S16] | **Core anchor:** shared practical curiosity. Avoid elaborate contraptions, endless time, or a nostalgic family ideal. |
| **Paddington — fictional** | Bond explains the character's courtesy, values, and disapproval of poor behavior [S15] | **Core anchor:** courtesy with a boundary. No scolding stare directed at customers, antique formality, or deliberate naïveté. |
| Captain Raymond Holt — fictional | Braugher describes a composed, taciturn character alongside status and petty rivalry [S17] | Reject as a default: deadpan can become distance or superiority. Short, precise sentences alone do not create care. |
| Leslie Knope — fictional | Poehler describes energetic optimism, honesty, and the difficulty of making change [S18] | Reject as a default: the brand should not mobilize every family moment into an improvement campaign. Sincerity remains useful. |
| Ted Lasso — fictional | Sudeikis describes curiosity instead of arrogance and a show that avoids snark as its currency [S19] | Supporting reference for a non-defensive question. Do not turn support into a pep talk or a reply into a coaching performance. |

### The six core anchors in practice

These references have separate jobs. Do not mix six personalities into one paragraph or assign imitation percentages. Use the technique, not a recognizable costume, catchphrase, or implied endorsement.

| Anchor | Borrow | Leave behind | Best use / failure condition | Original German adaptation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ralph Caspers | Name what can be observed; state the unknown plainly | Child-TV delivery and mandatory cleverness | Product explanation / fails when an irrelevant explanation delays the answer | „Die Bilder zeigen die Reihenfolge. Ob das bei euch hilft, wissen wir noch nicht.“ |
| Mai Thi Nguyen-Kim | Make the work behind a claim and its limits visible | Credentials as persuasion; the long explainer format | Evidence answer / fails when general research is made to sound like product proof | „Die Untersuchung betrifft Gespräche zwischen Eltern und Kindern. Unsere Karten wurden darin nicht untersucht.“ |
| Nora Imlau | Include the parent's capacity instead of assigning another task | Therapy vocabulary and certainty about a family's inner life | Optional editorial suggestion / fails when we give unasked-for parenting advice | „Wenn ihr gerade nichts ändern möchtet, lassen wir es dabei.“ |
| Chilli Heeler | Acknowledge an imperfect moment without making it a catastrophe | Perfect-parent availability or routine teasing of a partner | Light observational copy / fails when the reader is seeking serious help | „Die Jacken sind an. Der Plan hatte mit beiden Schuhen gerechnet.“ |
| Pettersson | Start with the thing at hand; let the child contribute an idea | Overengineered solutions and an endlessly available adult | A creative invitation / fails when free exploration is inappropriate or unsafe | „Wir haben ein Blatt Papier. Was möchtest du daraus machen?“ |
| Paddington | Keep courtesy while stating a limit | Moral correction, old-fashioned formality, naïveté | A polite refusal / fails if courtesy becomes a lecture | „Das können wir dir nicht versprechen. Wir können dir aber zeigen, was das Set enthält.“ |

The card-related lines above use the hypothetical card fixture from the benchmarks. The evidence line refers to the scope of the research, not proof of the fictional product. The paper invitation assumes a context approved for that activity. Every line remains subject to the real brief.

### Audience and communication research

- **S01 — Grosse Köpfe, first-person mental-load account (2018).** [Source][S01] · Authored parent account. The sequence of small tasks and repeated interruptions makes workload concrete. Informs V1–V2: use a recognizable detail and avoid adding needless work. Limitation: one public account; no prevalence or causal claim.
- **S02 — SPIEGEL/bento, parent interview about unwanted gifts (2019).** [Source][S02] · Original reported testimony. The parent balances gratitude, limited space, and reluctance to reject gifts. Informs V3 and B09: purchasing reluctance need not mean indifference. Only the parent's account is used; the article's developmental advice is not adopted as evidence.
- **S03 — Ganz normale Mama, unsolicited advice (2018).** [Source][S03] · First-person parent essay. A minor encounter prompts judgments based on a narrow view of family life. Informs V1 and section 6. Limitation: anecdotal and connected to an author's book promotion; not representative research or medical guidance.
- **S04 — Andreadakis, Joussemet and Mageau, supporting toddlers' autonomy (published online 2018).** [Source][S04] · Exploratory research. Parent reports identify practices including neutral problem descriptions and appropriate reasons. Informs V3 and V6. Limitation: correlational parent-report work with toddlers; does not validate German brand copy, causal outcomes, or a selba product.
- **S05 — Kunz and Grych, psychological control and autonomy granting (2013).** [Source][S05] · Observational research. Distinguishes these constructs rather than treating them as a single continuum. Informs V3: merely removing pressure does not automatically offer meaningful agency. Limitation: preadolescents and family interactions; extrapolation to this voice is explicitly editorial.

### Real people

- **S06 — News4teachers interview with Ralph Caspers (2020).** [Source][S06] · Original interview. He describes understanding before explaining, concrete topics, and admitting an unknown while identifying how to investigate it. Informs V2, V4 and the Caspers card. Limitation: broadcaster practice in child-directed explanation; not evidence of sales effectiveness.
- **S07 — fernsehserien.de interview with Ralph Caspers (2019).** [Source][S07] · Original interview. He rejects exaggerated, condescending child-directed presentation and acknowledges that his humor can divide audiences. Informs V5–V6 and the decision not to equate children with louder copy. Limitation: his experience and judgment, not a universal audience preference.
- **S08 — Fresh Air interview with Fred Rogers (2002).** [Source][S08] · Original transcript. The bathtub-drain discussion treats a particular fear concretely; a later answer declines an unsupported numerical generalization about parents. Informs V1 and V4. Limitation: historical parenting opinions are not adopted as current clinical recommendations.
- **S09 — DWDL interview with Mai Thi Nguyen-Kim and Ranga Yogeshwar (2018).** [Source][S09] · Original joint interview. Nguyen-Kim discusses consulting literature and experts, and earning attention rather than assuming it. Informs V2 and V4. Limitation: self-described professional method; comments by the two speakers must not be conflated.
- **S10 — Leben & Erziehen interview with Nora Imlau (2024).** [Source][S10] · Original author interview. Discusses parental pressure and the limits of a simple individual fix. Informs V3 and the parent-capacity anchor. Limitation: a book-related interview containing broader health and social claims; these claims and statistics are not imported as established evidence.
- **S11 — SPIEGEL interview with Loriot (1988).** [Source][S11] · Original interview. Locates comedy in misunderstanding and discusses the gap between courteous presentation and less gentle content. Informs V5's boundary against ridicule and unhelpful ambiguity. Limitation: cultural and historical context; selected mechanics do not endorse his broader views.
- **S12 — Bob Ross official biography (undated).** [Source][S12] · Interested official account. Presents accessible demonstrations, encouragement, and adaptation after mistakes.
