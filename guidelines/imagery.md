# Imagery guideline

**Let kids be kids. Show the world they are absorbed in.**

Our imagery gives form to a core ambition: confident children who have room to explore, try and do things themselves. We see them enjoying an activity, following an idea or making another attempt. Their attention belongs to what they are doing.

This guide covers lifestyle photography and images generated with a photographic appearance. Use it when briefing, making or selecting images. It develops the intent in [BRAND.md](../../BRAND.md); the [digital colour palette](../../assets/colors/digital.md) remains the source for colour values. Product specifications govern how a product may be depicted.

## 1. The moment we want to see

**The child leads the story.** Look for a choice, a gesture, an attempt or a moment of absorbed play. Confidence can be quiet: reaching for another pencil, returning to an unfinished construction or deciding where something belongs. A finished result is not required.

Children are occupied with activities they enjoy. They may be drawing, building, inventing a game, looking through a book or participating in an everyday routine. These are scene examples, not claims about what any selba product does.

Keep their gaze on the activity, the objects involved or another person in the scene. Do not ask them to look at the camera or hold up a result for the viewer. Expressions should feel unforced: concentration, curiosity, a small smile or quiet satisfaction. A concentrated face does not need to smile in every frame.

Their imaginary world comes through in their attention and play. We can see an ordinary room while understanding that, for the child, something absorbing is happening there.

**Parents and caregivers support.** They can be close, attentive and involved: holding something steady, offering a requested hand or sharing the child's interest. The child retains a meaningful action or choice. Avoid an adult demonstrating everything while the child watches, or a staged celebration that turns the child's activity into a performance. Support does not make a child less capable.

Set the age group and cast for the individual brief. Keep them consistent throughout the resulting series. The foundation's current audience planning focus does not replace the age group requested for an image brief.

## 2. A home with life in it

Set scenes **indoors**, in a recognisable, everyday German apartment. Aim for a slightly tidier and more colourful version of everyday home life, while keeping the small mismatches and things out of place that make it feel inhabited. The activity belongs in the room, and the room contains traces of life beyond this one photograph.

Avoid the spare, immaculate Nordic showroom look. The owner's comparison is useful: the room should feel a little more "off" and messy than an IKEA catalogue. Gently tidy enough to read the scene; leave enough irregularity to believe the family lives there. This describes the styling, not a required furniture brand or a single model of how German families live.

Keep the everyday clutter children carry on around: an open pencil case, a drink, a snack, a book from an earlier activity, a half-finished game, a sibling's things or a cushion pulled onto the floor. Choose details that make sense for the scene. These are possibilities, not a checklist to put into every image.

The mess should feel accumulated through use. Do not arrange every object so it faces the camera, space the props evenly or make the whole room immaculate around one carefully placed toy. Equally, do not pile on unrelated objects simply to signal chaos. We should still understand what the child is doing.

Leave room for ordinary posture and movement: knees tucked up, a body leaning over a drawing, a child shifting to reach something. Clothing, furniture and possessions should feel like they belong to these people in this home.

## 3. Light, colour and the three ranges

**Use Day when no product range is specified.** Use the chosen range to guide both the emotional pace and the visual atmosphere. Range colours belong naturally in clothes, textiles, furniture and everyday objects.

The palette is a reference for selecting and styling a scene. Preserve believable skin tones, material colours and the supplied product's actual appearance. Do not tint the whole photograph to match a swatch or recolour a product to fit a range.

For Morning and Day, favour natural window light, with visible detail in faces and a bright, clear feeling. Avoid an overall yellow or orange cast, exaggerated golden-hour warmth, harsh advertising-style highlights and heavy filters. Keep colour lively without making skin or materials look artificial.

The following direction applies the foundation's instruction to match energy to the moment through the three existing palettes based on [digital.md](/assets/colors/digital.md)

| Range | Mood and activity | Light and colour direction | Palette reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Morning** | Fresh, curious and gently energetic. Show the invitation to begin or take part, with time for the child to try. | Clear, fresh light. Yellow, pink, blue and lime give the scene lift; keep the energy welcoming rather than rushed. | `morning`, `morning-pink`, `morning-blue`, `morning-lime`|
| **Day — default** | Bright, playful and absorbed. Children have room to follow an idea and enjoy what they are doing. | Bright natural light with balanced whites. Green, blue, coral and purple support a lively everyday scene without exaggerated saturation. | `day`,`day-blue`,`day-coral`,`day-purple` |
| **Night** | Calm, reassuring and winding down. Show quiet participation and closeness at the child's pace. | Softer, lower-key indoor evening light with readable faces and activity. Navy creates depth; lavender, sage and sand soften the atmosphere. Keep warmth restrained and avoid an overall orange or blue cast. | `night`, `night-lavender`, `night-sage`, `night-sand` |

The shared primary colour is `primary`; the neutrals are `ink` and `screen`. They remain available within the wider brand palette. The selected range should guide the image; a scene does not need to display every swatch. Exact HEX values are digital references, not requirements for individual photographed pixels. Verify values against the linked palette when preparing a new brief.

## 4. The product belongs in the moment

Include the supplied selba product as a plausible part of the activity or surrounding home. The emotional point of the picture must remain clear before the viewer notices the product.

Give the product a believable position, scale and relationship to the people and objects around it. It may sit among other possessions or be involved in an activity when that use is verified. It should remain identifiable without being enlarged, isolated, spotlighted or held towards the camera. Do not clear away the scene simply to expose its label.

Preserve the actual product's shape, proportions, materials, colours and markings. Use the supplied product photographs as visual evidence; a photograph alone may not establish size, age suitability or how something works. Clarify any missing fact that would materially affect the depiction. Do not invent features, interactions or outcomes. If reference images show several different products, clarify whether the brief calls for them together or in separate series.

If the supplied material does not establish how the product is used, **ask for verified usage information before depicting interaction**. Do not infer a function from its appearance or name, and do not stage a child using it while that question remains unresolved.

A child's expression communicates the moment. It must not suggest an evidenced result from using the product where none has been established. Avoid transformation stories such as a frustrated child becoming confident because a product enters the scene.

## 5. Framing and a useful image series

Make the viewer feel close enough to notice the child's attention and the life around it. Use a clear focal point and enough environmental detail to understand the moment. Background softness can guide attention, but should not erase all the lived-in context.

Vary the distance and viewpoint across the series. A wide view establishes the apartment and activity; a medium view brings us closer to expression and gesture; a detail can show hands working while retaining a connection to the child and scene. A close view still tells a human story.

Frame around the activity rather than repeatedly placing the product in the centre. Keep hands and the meaningful part of the action readable. If a layout needs space for copy, find it in a quieter area of the real scene, without stripping the home of its character.

Create **eight separate images**, with a mix of **portrait and landscape** in every series. Include wider scenes, medium views and closer details across connected moments in the same apartment. Choose the balance of orientations for the intended uses; the set must offer both. Take exact dimensions and aspect ratios from the image brief when specified.

Within a series, keep the **same children and the same apartment**. Preserve faces, hair, ages, body proportions and the home's architecture, furnishings and recognisable belongings. Keep clothing consistent within a continuous episode; keep products unchanged. Props may move as the activity develops, but they should not randomly disappear, duplicate or change design.

Variation comes from different moments, framing and details within this shared world. Each image should contribute something useful; repeated near-identical frames do not make a useful series.

## 6. Approved visual reference

![Two children absorbed in drawing, surrounded by a pencil case, a cup, textiles and other belongings.](../../assets/imagery/reference.png)

*approved as a direction for attention, expression, colour, light and everyday clutter. The outdoor setting is an exception in this reference; selba scenes are indoors.*

| What to carry into selba imagery | What it means in practice |
| --- | --- |
| Attention on the activity | The children look down at what they are doing. The viewer is witnessing their moment. |
| Quiet enjoyment | Absorbed expressions, relaxed familiarity and room for a small smile; no performed excitement. |
| Bright, varied colour | Colourful clothes and belongings give the scene life. Adapt the colour relationships to the selected selba range. |
| Clear natural light | Keep the feeling of daylight and natural skin tones, without adding an overall warm filter. |
| Everyday clutter | The pencil case, cup, blanket and other belongings make the activity part of a fuller day. |
| Shared space | Another child can be involved or nearby without everyone performing the same gesture. |
| Products and objects within a scene | Objects have a place in the children's world; the emotional story stays with the children. |

Translate this feeling to an interior: for example, children absorbed in drawing on a living-room floor or at a family table, surrounded by the things already in use. This is an illustrative scene, not a prescribed room or activity.

The reference does not set casting, ages, clothing, an exact palette or selba product specifications. Its visible objects are not evidence of selba products. Use it to communicate the photographic direction.

## 7. Image review

| Look for | Revise when |
| --- | --- |
| A child absorbed in a meaningful activity | The child poses, looks at the camera or presents a product to the viewer. |
| Concentration and understated enjoyment | Every face has the same broad smile or exaggerated excitement. |
| A child with room to choose and try | The adult directs the entire moment or independence is staged as a test. |
| An indoor home with traces of daily life | The set feels empty, showroom-perfect or artificially cluttered. |
| Light and colour suited to the selected range | The wrong mood, a heavy colour cast or altered skin/product colours dominate. |
| A product that belongs in the scene | The picture reads as a product display, or product use has been invented. |
| A coherent series with distinct images | Children, room details or products change unexpectedly, or the views repeat. |

For generated imagery, also inspect faces, hands, bodies, contact between people and objects, product details, scale, shadows and reflections. Correct visible errors before selecting a final image. Review the images beside one another to catch continuity drift.

## 8. Reusable image-generation prompt

Provide the product photographs as actual image inputs, not only filenames. Provide this guideline `imagery.md`, `BRAND.md`, and `assets/colors/digital.md` alongside the images. The prompt below also carries the core direction for use on its own.

```text
Create a coherent series of 8 separate photographic lifestyle images for selba.

INPUTS
Product range: {{Morning / Day / Night}} — use Day if left blank.
Children's age group: {{AGE GROUP OF DEPICTED CHILDREN}}
Product reference images: {{ATTACHED IMAGE(S) OF THE PRODUCT TO INCLUDE}}
Delivery format: include both portrait and landscape images in this series.
Use any exact dimensions or aspect ratios specified in the image brief. If a
placement requires exact sizes and those are missing, ask before generating.

Use the supplied selba guideline, palette and approved style reference when
available. Product reference images define the product's appearance. The
approved lifestyle reference defines attention, expression, light and everyday
clutter; its outdoor setting does not carry over. All scenes must be indoors.

Before generating, ask for any missing required age group or product images.
Clarify product scale or identity if the references leave them ambiguous.
If they show different products, clarify whether to depict them together or
create separate series; do not mistake different views for different products.
If the product's use is unclear, ask for verified usage information before
depicting any interaction with it. Do not infer use from its appearance or
name. Resolve the relevant usage and age-suitability details before generating
a child handling or using the product; do not invent them.
Use the age group and casting details in the brief. If the cast size or any
required casting direction is missing, clarify it before establishing the
recurring children. Keep the same cast throughout the series.

THE STORY
Let kids be kids. Show children absorbed in an activity they enjoy, following
their own ideas in a lived-in apartment. Confidence appears in choosing,
trying, exploring and asking for help. Their gaze stays with the activity,
the objects involved or another person. Show concentration, curiosity and
quiet enjoyment, with a slight smile where it comes naturally. Do not make
every child smile. No looking at the camera, posed achievement, forced
excitement or presenting an object to the viewer.

Show imagination through behaviour and ordinary play in the physical room.
If a parent or caregiver appears, give them a supporting role and leave the
child a meaningful action or choice. Do not turn the activity into a lesson,
performance or demonstration led entirely by an adult.

THE HOME
Show a recognisable, everyday German apartment: a slightly tidier, more
colourful version of everyday home life, with small mismatches and things
out of place. Avoid a spare, immaculate Nordic showroom. Make it a little
more "off" and messy than an IKEA catalogue, without copying a catalogue
interior or making everything look deliberately disordered.
Keep believable traces of everyday family life around the activity: things
currently in use and a few remnants of what happened earlier. Depending on
the scene, this might include a drink, snack, open pencil case, another book,
unfinished game, a sibling's belongings or displaced cushions. Do not include
all of these by default. Keep the mess incidental and the activity readable.
Avoid a showroom, an empty set or an artfully arranged pile of props.

MOOD, LIGHT AND COLOUR
Use only the selected range as the main mood direction:
- Morning: fresh, curious and gently energetic; clear natural light.
- Day (default): bright, playful and absorbed; bright natural light with
  balanced whites.
- Night: calm, reassuring and winding down; softer, lower-key indoor evening
  light that keeps faces and activity readable.
Shared brand colours: primary, ink, screen.
Use the provided color palette as inspiration.

Bring range colours into believable clothes, textiles and objects; do not
require every swatch in every frame. Preserve natural skin, material and
product colours. No overall yellow/orange cast, blue wash, heavy filter or
artificial saturation. Keep any evening warmth restrained. Match the mood
through light, colour and activity, not by tinting the whole photograph.

PRODUCT
Include the supplied product in every image, naturally at rest or in verified
use. Keep it at a believable scale and preserve its form, proportions,
materials, colours and markings. It should be identifiable while the child's
activity and emotion remain the focal point. Do not enlarge it, spotlight it,
clear a display area around it or place its label towards the camera in every
frame. Do not invent product features, functions, accessories or outcomes.

SERIES AND CONTINUITY
Use the same children and the same apartment throughout. Establish their
appearance and the interior in the first image, then use that image as a
visual reference for the remaining images. Preserve facial features, ages,
hair, body proportions and clothing within this episode. Preserve room
geometry, windows, furniture, recurring belongings and product details.
Props can move logically as the activity develops. Do not redesign the home
or replace the children between images. A close crop need not show every
child, but any person shown must belong to this same cast.

Plan eight distinct, connected images suited to the range, age group and
verified product context. Use these shot roles:
1. Wide establishing view: the children, their activity and the inhabited room.
2. Another wide scene: a related moment in a different area of the same
   apartment, with recognisable continuity of people, home and belongings.
3. Medium view: a child's expression and hands engaged in the activity.
4. Medium interaction: shared activity with another cast member when present;
   for a single-child cast, show a distinct next action instead.
5. Over-the-shoulder view: the activity from close to the child's perspective.
6. Closer view: concentration and a small gesture, with enough context to keep
   the product naturally within the scene.
7. Detail within the activity: hands, materials and the product in its place;
   retain a human connection and avoid an isolated product display.
8. A connected next moment: the activity develops or changes at the child's
   pace, giving the series another usable story beat in this same apartment.
Adapt the actions to the brief. Do not force eight versions of the same pose.

Use a clear focal point, natural posture and enough context to understand the
activity. Keep meaningful gestures and hands readable. Vary the moment and
camera position without turning the sequence into unrelated scenes or
near-duplicates. Keep depth of field plausible and retain the feeling of a
home around the child.

DELIVERY AND REVIEW
Deliver 8 individual image files, not one collage or contact sheet.
If the tool works one image at a time, continue until the full series is
complete, reusing the first image and product references for continuity.
Check each result against the references and the series: faces and hands,
physical interactions, room continuity, product accuracy and scale, lighting,
palette and distinct framing. Revise errors and continuity drift before
delivery. Do not describe the generated scenes as real customer experiences
or evidence of product results.
```

## 9. Basis and external references

The brand decisions in this guide come from the owner's imagery brief and clarifications, [BRAND.md](../../BRAND.md), the [digital palette](../../assets/colors/digital.md) and the supplied reference image. Scene examples illustrate those decisions; they do not establish product facts.

Published guidelines informed the practical treatment of expression, setting, light, composition and series. They are references for those specific practices, not sources of additional selba brand rules:

- [Telenor — Brand Photography](https://brand.telenor.com/group/identity/imagery): observed moments, unforced expressions, everyday detail and devices remaining secondary to the human story.
- [University of Cambridge — Photography](https://www.cam.ac.uk/brand-resources/guidelines/photography): people absorbed in their own environments, natural light and a clear focal point with enough context.
- [Motorola Solutions — Photography](https://brand-guidelines.motorolasolutions.com/photography): a varied set of establishing, human-action and in-situation views; products can appear naturally in use or at rest when appropriate.
